# Particle system architecture

## Problem and design reasoning

A particle runtime must absorb bursts of short-lived objects, update them cheaply, and retire them without holding references to a destroyed gameplay owner. Shipped Quake dispatches transient network events into local particle effects. Bungie's Destiny 2 particle architecture supports authored parameter expressions evaluated on CPU or GPU, allowing fast editing while retaining scalable execution. The transferable inference is to separate gameplay's effect request from particle storage, evaluation, and rendering. Choose CPU or GPU ownership according to required interaction, readback cost, and workload. [id Software, Quake transient effects source](https://github.com/id-Software/Quake/blob/master/WinQuake/cl_tent.c) · [Brandon Whitley, The Destiny Particle Architecture, SIGGRAPH 2017](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2017/index.html) · [Whitley and Mayyasi, Visual Effects Technology in Destiny 2, GDC 2018](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc2018/presentations/Whitley_Brandon_Mayyasi_Ali_Visual_Effects_Technology.pdf)

## Techniques and practical checks

- **Separate definitions, emitters, and particles.** Store shared authored configuration in an immutable definition, instance parameters and random seed in an emitter, and position, age, and velocity in particle storage. Resolve transforms explicitly for world-space and owner-relative particles. **Check** two emitters sharing a definition, then remove one owner mid-effect. Verify the other emitter remains unchanged and the detached effect follows its documented lifetime.
- **Bound emission and allocation.** Use reusable storage with an explicit capacity and overflow policy, such as dropping decorative particles or prioritizing gameplay cues. Batch simulation by compatible update kernels and keep GPU counters separate from CPU counts when reading back would stall. **Check** a burst above capacity followed by several minutes of repeated spawning. Record allocation, dropped particles, and peak update cost, then confirm storage returns to the expected steady state.
- **Define event and clock semantics.** Give spawn requests a lifetime, clock domain, and event identity where replay can resend them. Route confirmed collision or gameplay events into effects and define whether simulation pause also pauses existing particles. **Check** pause, slow motion, replayed impact events, and a long frame. Verify a single confirmed hit creates one effect and particle lifetime follows the intended clock.
- **Keep bounds and retirement correct.** Derive conservative bounds for moving particles and mark a system complete only after emission stops and its live particles finish, or after an explicit forced termination. Respect GPU completion before recycling buffers. **Check** a fast emitter crossing the camera frustum, teleport, scene unload, and immediate pool reuse. Look for disappearing trails, stale particles, and references to recycled owners.
