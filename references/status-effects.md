# Buffs, debuffs, and status effects

## Problem and design reasoning

Timed effects can disagree with their icons, stack in an unexpected order, or survive a cleanse or respawn. In *Overwatch 2*, Kiriko's Protection Suzu combines brief invulnerability with removal of eligible negative effects. Blizzard's developers described giving Kiriko an active way to defend teammates. A later patch excluded most hard knockdowns while preserving the interaction with Ana's Sleep Dart. The transferable inference is to model prevention and removal independently and represent exceptions explicitly. [Blizzard, Kiriko design interview](https://overwatch.blizzard.com/en-us/news/23841483/let-the-kitsune-guide-you-a-first-look-at-kiriko-s-concept-and-playstyle/) · [Blizzard, June 2024 patch notes](https://overwatch.blizzard.com/en-us/news/patch-notes/live/2024/06/)

Blizzard's GDC talk on the original *Overwatch* describes the networking requirements for scripted weapons and abilities. That makes it a useful companion to status design when application, expiration, and player feedback must agree across clients. The policies below are implementation recommendations derived from the design problem. [Dan Reed, Networking Scripted Weapons and Abilities in Overwatch, GDC 2017](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc2017/Presentations/Reed_Dan_NetworkingScriptedWeapons.pdf)

## Techniques and practical checks

- **Specify effect identity and lifetime.** Store effect type, source, target, application time, duration, and periodic cadence. State the governing clock and whether a tick at expiry is included. **Check** an effect applied immediately before a tick, one ending on a tick boundary, and one crossing pause or respawn. Verify exact tick counts and icon removal.
- **Choose one reapplication policy per effect.** Decide whether applications refresh time, extend time up to a cap, add magnitude, or stay independent by source. Give modifiers a fixed evaluation order and describe that order where players need it. **Check** repeated application by one source and two sources, at and above the cap, in both arrival orders. Verify stats, duration, and indicated stacks against the rule.
- **Resolve prevention, suppression, and removal explicitly.** Use effect categories to describe which incoming effects immunity blocks, what temporary suppression leaves attached, and which active effects a cleanse or dispel may remove. Define same-tick ordering for incoming effects and removal. **Check** a blocked application, a cleansable debuff, an exempt debuff, and a positive buff targeted by a dispel. Repeat with simultaneous applications and verify who keeps each effect.
- **Derive feedback from resolved state.** Drive icons, remaining time, and cues from committed state. In multiplayer, replicate the authoritative result and correct any predicted presentation. **Check** a repeated effect message, late arrival during an active effect, and removal while the client is delayed. Verify a single cue per committed transition and an accurate remaining time.
