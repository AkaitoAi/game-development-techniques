# Runtime texture generation

## Problem and design reasoning

Rebuilding a whole texture for a small paint stroke or material edit can turn a cheap gameplay action into a large upload and a visible hitch. Ubisoft's shipped *Far Cry 4* generates cached terrain and decal textures as part of its adaptive virtual texture system. Ka Chen's GDC 2015 presentation documents the runtime rendering and compression work. This is useful evidence for amortizing material construction, while the general texture update problem also applies to paint masks, minimaps, and procedural surfaces. [Ubisoft, Adaptive Virtual Texture Rendering in Far Cry 4, GDC 2015](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1021761/) · [Ka Chen's presentation](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc2015/presentations/Chen_Ka_AdaptiveVirtualTexture.pdf)

Ubisoft's *Ghost Recon Wildlands* terrain presentation describes generating tiles into offscreen surfaces and compressing them with asynchronous compute. Fast traversal could request enough tiles to cause spikes, so the team distributed updates across frames. The portable recommendation is to give generated texture revisions explicit ownership and bounded publication work. This recommendation extends the documented approach beyond its terrain cache. [Ubisoft, Ghost Recon Wildlands Terrain Technology and Tools, GDC 2017](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc2017/Presentations/WERLE_MARTINEZ_GRWterrainTechnologyTools.pdf)

## Techniques and practical checks

- **Track dirty regions and revision identity.** Record which regions changed and which source revision produced an update. Merge nearby regions when the saved call overhead exceeds the additional transfer cost. Respect compressed block boundaries, row pitch, texture format, and the destination mip. **Check** single-pixel edits, edits crossing region boundaries, and overlapping edits arriving before a previous upload completes. Compare against a full rebuild and verify that the latest revision wins.
- **Publish complete results at a defined boundary.** Stage CPU output or render into a separate GPU resource when readers need a coherent image. Publish the resulting resource or revision after the required writes are complete, then retire the previous storage only after its consumers finish. **Check** generating an update during a camera cut, scene unload, and resource resize. Verify that no frame samples partially written or retired data.
- **Budget generation and upload separately.** Measure CPU generation, render or compute work, transfer bytes, and mip creation independently. Prioritize visible changes and retain a usable previous image when a request exceeds the current frame budget. **Check** a burst of edits and rapid traversal into uncached content. Record queue age, peak staging memory, and frame-time tails, then confirm that the queue eventually drains.
- **Regenerate dependent mips and borders.** Define how an edit affects neighboring filter footprints and lower mip levels. Use appropriate color-space handling and regenerate only the dependencies that the format and filter require. **Check** bright edits beside dark unchanged texels at several distances and oblique viewing angles. Inspect seams, stale lower mips, and edge bleeding against the full-rebuild reference.
