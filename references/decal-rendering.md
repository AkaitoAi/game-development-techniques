# Decal rendering

## Problem and design reasoning

Localized markings such as road strips, labels, scuffs, and impact effects need to follow the receiving material and lighting without requiring unique texture assets for every marked surface. Projection can reuse a decal across many shapes, but can bleed onto adjacent geometry and increases per-pixel work when layers overlap. A fitted mesh gives the artist control over the silhouette and receiver. It adds geometry and needs an explicit policy for depth conflicts and moving receivers.

Guerrilla documented decals that modify part of the G-buffer to reuse the receiving surface's lighting. id Software documented both geometric and projected decals as G-buffer modifications before lighting, with a separate update that writes only touched channels. These cases support an engineering inference: receiver shape, affected channels, and expected screen coverage should determine the representation and pass placement. [Michal Valient, Deferred Rendering in Killzone 2, Develop Brighton 2007](https://www.guerrilla-games.com/media/News/Files/Develop07_Valient_DeferredRenderingInKillzone2.pdf) · [id Software, Visibility Buffer and Deferred Rendering in Doom: The Dark Ages, Graphics Programming Conference 2025](https://static.graphicsprogrammingconference.com/public/2025/talks/visibility-buffer-and-deferred-rendering-in-doom-the-dark-ages/Lazarek-Hammer-visibility-buffer-and-deferred-rendering-in-doom-the-dark-ages.pdf)

## Shipped-game examples

- **Killzone 2.** Guerrilla's Develop Brighton presentation illustrates decals for posters, cracks, and stains. Their pass blends into the albedo buffer, preserving the underlying lighting response. The slides also describe an intensity channel used to address accumulation. [Michal Valient, Develop Brighton 2007](https://www.guerrilla-games.com/media/News/Files/Develop07_Valient_DeferredRenderingInKillzone2.pdf)
- **Doom: The Dark Ages.** id Software's shipped renderer combines geometric and projected decals with other surface updates in its deferred path. The presentation shows the scene with each decal class enabled in turn and documents an option to separate material updates from lighting. [id Software, Graphics Programming Conference 2025](https://static.graphicsprogrammingconference.com/public/2025/talks/visibility-buffer-and-deferred-rendering-in-doom-the-dark-ages/Lazarek-Hammer-visibility-buffer-and-deferred-rendering-in-doom-the-dark-ages.pdf)

## Techniques and practical checks

- **Constrain projection to the intended receiver.** Transform each candidate receiver position into the decal's local frame and reject positions beyond its bounds. Limit receiver angle or material class where projection would otherwise wrap onto unwanted surfaces. Use a fitted mesh when authored contour control matters. **Check** a shallow plane, inside corner, nearby second wall, and the back of a thin wall. Inspect for leakage and seams at grazing angles.
- **Choose which material channels the mark changes.** Define whether a decal alters color, normal, roughness, or a subset. Apply material edits before lighting when possible and specify overlap order for noncommutative blends. Normalize blended normals in a common coordinate space. **Check** color-only and normal-only decals separately under a light that moves across them, then overlap two decals in both authoring orders.
- **Bind transient marks to the right lifetime.** Pick receiver-local or world placement based on whether the mark travels with the surface. Define removal on receiver destruction, region unload, expiration, or budget eviction. **Check** a decal on a moving door, then unload and reload the region. Verify artifacts clear or restore according to the feature's persistence contract.
- **Budget bounds and overlap.** Cull by view and distance, tighten boxes to rendered content, and measure pixels covered and layers per pixel in addition to count. **Check** one large decal and many small ones, then a stack of overlapping decals in a dense scene. Compare CPU submission and GPU pass time while toggling the decal group.
