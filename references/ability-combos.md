# Ability combos

## Problem and design reasoning

A combo lets one action set up an extra outcome for a later action. Players need to recognize when their setup is ready, which follow-up qualifies, and what happens if several eligible setups overlap. Without a consistent resolution rule, a teammate can change the result just by placing another effect nearby.

- **Guild Wars 2.** ArenaNet updated combo finishers to prefer the player's own eligible field and added a highlight to finisher icons when the player stands in a combo field. The patch also updated field visuals. These are documented changes. A transferable inference is that predictable ownership rules and a cue at the point of action help players plan even when several teammates share the same space. [ArenaNet, August 17, 2021 game update notes](https://en-forum.guildwars2.com/topic/99823-game-update-notes-august-17-2021/)
- **Divinity: Original Sin 2.** Larian's environment interaction guide illustrates heroes combining elemental actions, including one hero applying oil and another using fire. At GDC 2019, Swen Vincke described an iteration-led production process and recommended investing in feedback loops and testing in representative conditions. A design inference for combos is to validate opportunities in full encounters, where turns, terrain, and competing actions affect whether players can use them. [Larian, environment interaction](https://larian.com/support/faqs/what-is-environment-interaction_35) · [Swen Vincke, The Making of Divinity: Original Sin 2, GDC 2019](https://gdcvault.com/play/1025734/The-Making-of-Divinity-Original)

## Techniques and practical checks

- **Represent setup and payoff explicitly.** Specify the eligible source, target or area, duration, trigger, and resulting effect. Decide whether the payoff consumes, transforms, or leaves the setup available. Keep base ability behavior readable when no combination applies. **Check** a trigger without setup, one with a valid setup, and one after the setup expires. Repeat the trigger and verify the intended consumption policy.
- **Resolve competing combinations consistently.** Evaluate eligibility at a defined moment such as impact. Use an explicit rule to rank eligible setups and a stable tie-breaker when priority is equal. Bound triggered chains by declaring which outcomes may trigger further effects and at what depth. **Check** overlapping setups, two follow-ups in one simulation tick, and a reaction that could trigger itself. Confirm the same ordered outcome on repeated runs.
- **Track both contributors in co-op.** Record the setup provider and the payoff activator separately. Define which actor's stats drive the effect and how the game credits assists, score, or objectives. Where the server owns simulation, derive credit from its confirmed result. **Check** two teammates combining across simulated latency, including a setup provider who disconnects before the result. Confirm that both contributions appear as intended and the payoff is awarded once.
- **Teach the opportunity where players choose.** Show compatible setup and payoff tags in ability details. Add a live cue for an eligible follow-up and separate feedback for the resolved outcome. Include a low-pressure encounter that makes the interaction easy to try. **Check** whether a new player can predict and reproduce the combo after one encounter, then repeat with reduced visual effects and muted audio.
