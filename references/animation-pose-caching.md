# Animation pose caching

## Problem and design reasoning

Rendering, attachments, and hitboxes can ask for the same character's bones during one frame. Evaluating the whole animation again for each consumer wastes work, while reusing a pose after a model or parameter change gives inconsistent results. Valve's Source SDK, used for shipped games such as *Half-Life 2* and *Team Fortress 2*, records a bone setup time, a model-bone counter, and readable bone masks. Its model-change path invalidates the bone cache and rebuilds dependent storage. [Valve, C_BaseAnimating implementation](https://github.com/ValveSoftware/source-sdk-2013/blob/master/src/game/client/c_baseanimating.cpp)

Naughty Dog's GDC 2011 presentation describes growing *Uncharted 2*'s NPC population while improving animation CPU and memory efficiency. That establishes the production problem of serving more character animation within a budget. The cache protocols below are portable recommendations, and the talk summary does not establish that Naughty Dog used these specific keys or eviction rules. [Naughty Dog, GDC 2011 animation presentations](https://www.naughtydog.com/blog/gdc_2011_day_two)

## Techniques and practical checks

- **Define what the cached pose represents.** Store its rig revision, source tick or sample time, evaluated bone set, and the revisions of inputs that can affect it. Choose whether the cache holds local transforms before IK, world transforms after IK, or another explicit stage. Keep per-character contact corrections outside a shared pose unless their inputs are also represented. **Check** request rendering bones followed by attachment and hitbox bones in the same frame, then change a pose parameter. Verify that each consumer receives the required bones from the correct evaluation stage.
- **Publish a complete immutable result.** Give one job ownership of a cache entry while building it, then expose the completed result to readers. Retain its clip and rig resources until readers finish. Coalesce requests for an identical entry when that costs less than duplicate evaluation. **Check** run several consumers concurrently and delay one producer. Confirm that readers see either the previous permitted result or the complete current result and that a model replacement cannot publish into the old entry.
- **Invalidate on meaningful changes.** Include model, skeleton, blend inputs, playback discontinuities, and any world-space dependencies relevant to the chosen cache stage. Invalidate descendant data such as attachments when their base pose changes. A frame number alone is sufficient only when all relevant inputs remain fixed during that frame. **Check** teleport, scrub time backward, replace a rig, change an IK target, and alter an animation layer between two requests. Compare with an uncached evaluation using per-bone position and rotation tolerances.
- **Measure cache cost as well as saved work.** Track hit rate, pose bytes, allocation churn, lookup cost, and sampling avoided. Bound entries and define retirement after all consumers release them. **Check** compare a repeated crowd animation with characters using distinct blend states and frequently changing rigs. Verify improvement in total animation time and memory peaks, and inspect evictions for repeated evaluation spikes.
