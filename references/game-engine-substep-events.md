# Physics substeps and gameplay events

## Problem and design reasoning

When physics advances more often than gameplay or rendering, a body can enter and leave a trigger between two gameplay reads. A single impact can also generate several contact observations. Using the final overlap state loses short transitions, while reacting to each observation can repeat an effect. In *Rocket League*, Psyonix used a fixed 120 Hz physics tick. Jared Cone explains that the smaller steps made hits more consistent at greater simulation and correction cost. The gameplay inference is to choose event semantics independently of the frequency needed by the solver. Epic documents one concrete delivery contract in Unreal Engine 4.27, where collision callbacks are queued until the final substep and ordered by substep. [Jared Cone, It IS Rocket Science!, GDC 2018](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc2018/presentations/Cone_Jared_It_Is_Rocket.pdf) · [Epic, physics sub-stepping and callback delivery](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/physics-sub-stepping?application_version=4.27)

## Techniques and practical checks

- Record a compact observation with its simulation tick, substep, pair identifiers, and transition or contact data. Drain records at a defined gameplay boundary, retaining transition order when the action depends on it. **Check** move an object completely through a thin trigger during one gameplay update. Verify the expected enter and exit sequence even though the object is outside at delivery.
- Define how observations become gameplay events. For example, an objective may care about an entry edge while a sound effect may use the strongest impact for a pair in one window. Keep these policies close to the consumer. **Check** run a bouncing object and a resting contact under several substep counts. Compare effect counts, threshold crossings, and the duration of any persistent overlap.
- Keep solver callbacks short. Copy the data needed by gameplay, then resolve object handles after the step and mutate the world only at the engine’s allowed boundary. **Check** despawn an object between observation and dispatch and recycle its slot. Confirm the old observation cannot act on the replacement.
- Give replayable observations stable identities within the simulation timeline. Commit irreversible side effects at the confirmed boundary where prediction is used. Bound queue storage and report overflow explicitly. **Check** replay a tick after correction and then stress the busiest scene. Confirm gameplay outcomes reproduce, irreversible effects occur once, and queue pressure is visible in diagnostics.
