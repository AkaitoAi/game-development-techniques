# Game animation root motion

## Problem and design reasoning

An authored step, pivot, or vault can look convincing while missing its landing point or sliding through a blocker. Root motion turns animation displacement into a movement request. The gameplay movement system can then decide how much displacement to accept, and presentation can account for any difference. Define which system owns translation and turning in each state so animation and gameplay cannot both apply the same motion.

In the GDC talk *Motion Matching in The Last of Us Part II*, Naughty Dog describes an authored Align node that drives the capsule and provides the frame in which joints are evaluated. The talk explains why its accuracy matters to the animation system. Ubisoft's *Learned Motion Matching* paper describes the system developed for *For Honor* and includes root translation and rotation velocities in its motion representation. These cases support making the character trajectory explicit. A transferable inference is to choose source and ownership per movement state, such as controller-driven free movement and animation-driven displacement during a committed traversal action. [Michal Mach and Maksym Zhuravlov, GDC slides](https://media.gdcvault.com/GDC%2B2021/Motion_Matching_In_TLOU2.pdf) · [Ubisoft La Forge, Learned Motion Matching](https://static-wordpress.ubisoft.com/montreal.ubisoft.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/09154101/Learned_Motion_Matching.pdf)

## Implementation and checks

1. Establish where the root lives and what space its deltas use. For column vectors and transforms mapping root-local coordinates into clip space, the relative delta is `inverse(RootAtStart) * RootAtEnd`. Compose intervals across loop wraps and clip segments, and apply the resulting movement once in the controller's frame. **Check** sample a turning loop across its seam at several update rates and compare the composed displacement against the authored track. Include a mirrored clip and an actor spawned facing a different direction.
2. Keep motion extraction separate from collision resolution. Let the movement system sweep or constrain the requested change, and carry accepted displacement into the final pose. Set an explicit policy for blocked actions, such as stopping, continuing in place, or transitioning to recovery. **Check** run the same clip on level ground, a slope, a moving platform, and against a blocker. Trace the requested and accepted position and yaw. Confirm a blocked delta cannot accumulate into a jump when the obstruction clears.
3. Make handoffs and target adjustments bounded. Define which translation and rotation channels transfer ownership during a blend, and give target alignment a maximum distance and angle. Reject or recover if the endpoint becomes infeasible. **Check** interrupt a turning vault at early, contact, and late frames. Verify the controller resumes with a consistent pose and velocity and that clearing or moving the target does not pull the character through a wall.
4. Keep the movement clock and correction policy explicit. If root motion affects simulation, sample it at authoritative steps or consume timestamped deltas exactly once. Presentation can interpolate accepted state between steps. **Check** replay identical authoritative commands while changing render rate and inserting a deliberate frame hitch. Compare accepted displacements, controller pose, and completion timing at the same simulation ticks.
